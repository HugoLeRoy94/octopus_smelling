## Notations 

- $k_\text{sub}$: number of sub-units in a receptor (typically 5)
- $n_\text{unit}$: number of possible units / number of genese (typically 26)
- $\mathcal{U} = (u_1,u_2,\cdots u_{k_\text{sub}})$: identity of a detector, where $u_i \in [1,n_\text{unit}]$ (homo-pentamers looks like (1,1,1,1,1)).
- $r$: index / identity of a receptor
- $\ell$: index / identity of a ligand
- $\alpha$: index for open/close or leakiness sometimes.
- $K_\alpha^{(r,\ell)}$: dissociation constant of the channel in the $\alpha$ state, between a receptor $r$ and a ligand $\ell$
- $\Delta E_\alpha^{(r,\ell)} = E_\text{bound} - E_\text{unbound}$: energy difference between the bound and unbound state.
- $\epsilon_r = E_c - E_o$: energy difference between the closed and opened state of the channel without ligand. Leakiness $=e^\epsilon$
- $\theta = \{(\Delta E_\alpha^{(r,\ell)})_{\alpha,\ell}, \epsilon_r\}_r = \{\theta_r\}_r$ generic name for the characteristics of a channel
- $\mu^\alpha_r$, $\sigma^\alpha_r$: respectively the mean and std deviation of the distribution from which the energies are drawn.
- $w$ generic name for $\mu^\alpha_r$ and $\sigma^\alpha_r$.
- $N$: total number of receptors in the array.
- $\mathcal{A} = (a_0,a_1, \cdots , a_N)$: activity value of the array of receptors.

## Vocabulary

- Receptor: a single ion channel.
- Unit: a single protein that constitue a receptor.
- ligand: an arbitrary molecule.
- Pocket: the region of the unit within which a ligand bind.
- Residue: a piece of molecule that composes the pocket or/and a ligand.


## 1. Introduction

Our research investigates the generation of functional diversity in octopus olfactory channels, which are understood to be pentamers ($k_\text{sub}=5$) formed from a pool of $n_\text{unit} = 26$ different proteins (e.g., 'CR518', 'CR918', etc.). We hypothesize that an olfactory cell's response to a ligand is not from a single, defined channel type but rather from a combinatorial ensemble of all possible pentameric stoichiometries and combinatories (e.g., AAAAA, ABBBB, AABBB, ABABB etc..) based on the genes it expresses. 
We want to **measure the diversity of resposne generated by combining hetero-pentamers**

### Experimental procedure

We will fit the experimental dose-response curves for the homopentamers 'CR518' and 'CR918', as well as the heteropentameric 'CR518\_918' combination, across a panel of 11 ligands (including "Medroxyprogesterone", "Androsterone", "Progesterone", etc.).
-> Experimental dose-response curves measure the current generated by the diffusion of small ions through a membrane.
-> This current is proportional to the opening probability of the channels embedded in the membrane.
-> To rule-out the influence of this proportionality factor, we focus our fitting procedure on the renormalize current:
$$
\text{renormalized current} = \frac{I(c) - I_\text{min}}{I_\text{max}-I_\text{min}} = \frac{p_\text{open}(c) - p_\text{min}}{p_\text{max}-p_\text{min}}
$$

### Physical model of a channel

Each receptor is made of $k_\text{sub}$ sub-units among $n_\text{unit}$ possible units.
We denote $\bm{r} = (r_0 \cdots r_{k_\text{sub}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_\text{unit} \times k_\text{sub}}$ the identity of a receptor characterized by the identity of each of the units that composes it. 
The response of a receptor $\bm{r}$ to a ligand $\ell$ at concentration $c$ can be written using the MWC model for ion channel opening:
$$
p_o^r(c,\ell) = \frac{\prod_{u\in \mathcal{U}} (1+c/K_o^{(u,\ell)})}{\prod_{u\in \mathcal{U}} (1+c/K_o^{(u,\ell)}) + e^{-\epsilon}\prod_{u\in \mathcal{U}}(1+c/K_c^{(u,\ell)})}
$$
Where the $K$'s are called the affinities and defined by:
$$
K_o^{(r,\ell)} = \exp\left[ \Delta E_o^{r,\ell} \right]
$$
$$
K_c^{(r,\ell)} = \exp\left[ \Delta E_c^{r,\ell} \right]
$$
Each affinity correspond to the response of a sub-unit to a ligand.

Previous work on drosophilia [10.1038/srep21841] and humang [10.1038/sdata.2015.2] showed that the overall response of an array of ligand can be approximated to a Gaussian distrib. However, individual receptors display a wide variety of response, some very narrow, other broader and Gaussian.
-> In general, the microscopic interaction that compose the affinity between a ligand and a receptor is a finite pool (Van der Waals, aromatic interactions, electrostatic interactions, hydrophobic interactions).
-> This leads the interaction energies between a given receptor and different ligands to be correlated. For instance, a polar receptor should have affinity for every polar ligands.
-> Instead of making a complete, microscopic model of each receptor and ligands based on the specific microscopic interaction, we build affinities values by sorting the response of a given receptor with respect to a familly of ligands (eg: acids, alcohol, aldehydes, esters, aromatics, terpenes etc...)
-> In summarize, this approach accounts for the correlation within a family, and neglect the inter-family correlation.
-> Following previous work on drosophilia [10.1038/srep21841] and humang [10.1038/sdata.2015.2], we model the distribution of interaction energy between a family and given receptor as Gaussian.
-> This can be interepreted as a consequence of the central limit theorem, where the interaction energy represents an effective average of many microscopic interactions.



### Information of an Odor Signal

-> Here, we uses concepts from information theory to explore the coding capacity of chemoreceptors and to determine if assembling receptors from different subunits (heteromers) genuinely creates new diversity.

-> The core idea is to measure the **Mutual Information (MI)** between the ligands ($\ell$ from a pool of $L$ possible ligands) and the receptor activation patterns ($b(c_\ell) = \{a_0(c_\ell),\cdots,a_K(\ell)\}$) at concentration $c_\ell$. Considering that the activation curve of the receptors provide a binary signal: activated or not-activated, the activation pattern $b$ becomes a binary word with $K$ digits, where $K$ is the number of receptors. In the following, we assume that $2^K>L$, which means that there are enough receptors to encode each ligand uniquely.

* **Mutual Information** is defined as $MI(L; b) = H(b) - H(b|L)$.
    * $H(b)$ is the **entropy** of the activation patterns
    * $H(b|L)$ is the **conditional entropy**


First consdering **Deterministic Binding:** In an ideal, deterministic scenario, each ligand ($\ell$) always activates one specific pattern ($b^*(c_\ell)$). In this case, knowing the ligand means there is zero uncertainty about the pattern, so $H(b|L) = 0$.
When $H(b|L) = 0$, the mutual information $MI(L; b) = H(b)$. The maximum possible value for $H(b)$ (if all $2^K$ patterns are possible) is $K$. Therefore, the maximum information "capacity" of the system is $K$ bits.
We now explore the effect of the coupling between receptors on the mutual information, and more precisely on the capacity of the channel.

**Model of Redundancy:** Imagine the $k$-th receptor's activation in response to a concentration $c_\ell$ of ligand $\ell$ ($a_k(c_\ell)$) is determined by the others: $a_k(c_\ell) = f(a_1(c_\ell), ..., a_{k-1}(c_\ell))$. As a consequence Because the $k$-th bit is fixed, the total number of *possible* activation patterns $b$ is no longer $2^K$. It is reduced to $2^{K-1}$n and the entropy of the observed patterns $H(b)$ is now reduced to $\log_2(2^{K-1}) = K-1$.
  
**Functional Redundancy:** Considering a heteropentamer made of two type of sub-unit: $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Knowing the activity of two homopentamer at a given concentration, is not sufficient to infer the activity of the heteropentamer. Instead, the whole function of the homopentamers are required to find the activity of the hetero one:
$$
a_{\alpha,\beta}(c_\ell) = F\left[a_\alpha(c_\ell),a_\beta(c_\ell) \right] \hspace{1cm} \forall \ell
$$

### 3. Application to Heteromers (Page 17)

This reasoning is applied to test whether heteromers (receptors built from different subunits) provide *true* new information or are just redundant.

* [cite_start]**Null Hypothesis:** A heteromer brings **no additional information** if its response parameters ($\vec{\phi}^{Hetero}$) can be perfectly predicted by a fixed function $f$ of the parameters of its constituent homomers ($\vec{\phi}^{Homo1}$, $\vec{\phi}^{Homo2}$)[cite: 391, 396, 404].
* [cite_start]**Critical Condition:** This redundancy only holds if that function $f$ is **the SAME for all ligands**[cite: 395].
* [cite_start]**Conclusion:** If the heteromer's response is just a fixed combination of the homomer responses (for all ligands), it is redundant, just like the $k$-th receptor in the previous example[cite: 396]. It doesn't add to the system's information capacity. [cite_start]True diversity ($a_k \neq f(a_1...)$) requires that the heteromer responds to ligands in a way that *cannot* be predicted by its parts[cite: 385].
